Technical detail
gemma-4-31B-it at 32k tokens, fp8 KV cache: serves users from 1× H100 NVL 94GB, or 1× GH200 96GB. With 1× H100 NVL 94GB:
20 users guaranteed, 20 likely, at 40 tok/s (31 to 49, ±23%, estimated) each; reading 32k tokens: 3.6 s (2.3 to 5.0 s,
±37%).
Scenario "documents": each request brings 16k new tokens to read and generates 500; at least 20 tok/s per user. For a
chat (the conversation stays in the cache) use --prompt-cache 80.

  guaranteed = holds even if the real speed is at the low end of the estimate; likely = at the central estimate.
  GPUs: "4 (1 model on 4)" or "4 (1×4)" = one copy of the model split across 4 GPUs that work together on every token
  (tensor parallel, TP); "8 (2 copies × 4)" or "8 (2×4)" = 2 independent copies, each on 4 GPUs and serving its own
  users; "split in 2 stages" = each GPU holds part of the layers (pipeline parallel, PP); "2 stages of 4" = each stage
  on 4 GPUs in tensor parallel; "2 copies × 2 stages of 2" or "2×2 stages of 2" = 2 independent copies, each split in 2
  stages of 2 GPUs. One server holds up to 8 GPUs (RTX 3090, RTX 4090, RTX 5080, RTX 5090, RTX A6000, RTX 6000 Ada, RTX
  PRO 6000 WS, RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q: 4; GH200 96GB, GH200 144GB: 2).

(1) nobody is guaranteed 20 tok/s of writing, even with 4 GPUs in one server
(2) TP 4 on cards without NVLink or P2P: every all-reduce goes through CPU memory over PCIe (slower; needs x16 slots and
    an adequate power supply)
(3) partly supported: FA2 does not support fp8 KV, so vLLM selects another attention backend (FLASHINFER for most
    models, with Q in bf16). There are open bugs on this path: with pip and no nvcc, FlashInfer is selected but does not
    compile (crash 'Could not find nvcc'), and with checkpoints that have q_scale the output is wrong. Workaround:
    --attention-backend TRITON_ATTN (source: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/60262)
(4) with 2 GPUs the model fits, but nobody is guaranteed 20 tok/s of writing: 4 GPUs serve someone
(5) 4 GPUs in one group, but NVLink connects only 2 of these cards: the group talks over PCIe (slower)
(6) TP 4 over PCIe (no NVLink): all-reduces slower than over NVLink
(7) with 1 GPU the model fits, but nobody is guaranteed 20 tok/s of writing: 2 GPUs serve someone
(8) TP 8 over PCIe (no NVLink): all-reduces slower than over NVLink

Memory per GPU with one request of 32k tokens (≈ 25,000 words ≈ 49 pages): the model's weights and its conversation
memory (KV cache) are computed exactly, the engine's fixed costs are estimated. Margin = room left for more
conversations after that request. Card memory = what CUDA reports (the engine's usable memory; nvidia-smi shows a little
more).
Users: total = concurrent requests ÷ 10% active at once (--active); each gets ≥20 tok/s and the first token within 10 s
(or, if longer, twice the wait for the first words of one typical request alone: half the context, less what the prefix
cache holds).
Accuracy: exact = computed; measured = this model on this card; calibrated = fitted on measurements of this card or of
the same model type (N points); estimated = calibrated on other cards, not verified, or extrapolated; not precise enough
to decide = ±50% or more, or a case not covered.
Speed basis (vLLM 0.31.0) of the cards shown: fitted on 43 public vLLM benchmarks (dense models, MoE in MXFP4 on H100 /
H200 / B200), vLLM 0.31.0 (43 points); measured on RTX 5090 with vLLM 0.31.0: Qwen3-8B BF16 (whole, and cut to 2 layers
from 64K to 1M); 18 points, on Windows / WSL 2 (18 points).
Weights: exact as the sum of the tensors; for BF16 × multimodal vLLM measured up to +1.8% more, counted in the fixed
costs.
Multimodal model: with --text-only (vLLM --language-model-only) the vision encoder is not loaded.
