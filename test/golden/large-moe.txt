Summary
  Recommendation: 1 server with 2× NVIDIA B200 SXM serves 60 users guaranteed, 60 likely, with requests of up to 32k
      tokens (≈ 25,000 words ≈ 49 pages; 16k on average).
  Alternative: 1 server with 2× NVIDIA B300 SXM (fits; 90 users guaranteed, 160 likely).
  Not enough: L4, even with 8 GPUs in one server; RTX A6000, RTX 6000 Ada and consumer cards, even with 4; GH200, even
      with 2.
  Speed for each user: writing about 32 words per second (19 to 45; 43 tokens/s); with everyone busy the first words
      appear after about 0.36 s, and a full 32k-token request alone is read in about 0.82 s (1 token ≈ 0.75 words).
  Confidence: memory high (±1.6%); writing speed medium (±40%); reading time low, not precise enough to decide (more
      than ±50%); user count medium (±16%). Plan on the guaranteed figures.

Technical detail
Hy3-FP8 at 32k tokens, fp8 KV cache: serves users from 2× B200 SXM (1 model on 2), or 2× B300 SXM (1 model on 2). With
2× B200 SXM (1 model on 2): 60 users guaranteed, 60 likely, at 43 tok/s (26 to 60, ±40%, estimated) each; reading 32k
tokens: 0.82 s (0.39 to 1.3 s, ±53%).
Scenario "documents": each request brings 16k new tokens to read and generates 500; at least 20 tok/s per user. For a
chat (the conversation stays in the cache) use --prompt-cache 80.

(1) TP 4 over PCIe (no NVLink): all-reduces slower than over NVLink
(2) partly supported: Starts natively on FlashInferFP8ScaledMMLinearKernel (bmm_fp8 backend auto), but open issues show
    its autotuned cuDNN FP8 GEMM causing illegal memory access or engine wedges under sustained load with CUDA graphs on
    SM120 (RTX PRO 5000/6000). Workaround: VLLM_DISABLED_KERNELS=FlashInferFP8ScaledMMLinearKernel (selection then falls
    to CutlassFP8ScaledMMLinearKernel); --enforce-eager also avoided it in #55571. FA2 does not support fp8 KV, so vLLM
    selects another attention backend (FLASHINFER for most models, with Q in bf16). There are open bugs on this path:
    with pip and no nvcc, FlashInfer is selected but does not compile (crash 'Could not find nvcc'), and with
    checkpoints that have q_scale the output is wrong. Workaround: --attention-backend TRITON_ATTN (source:
    https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.31.0/vllm/utils/flashinfer.py#L1149-L1160)
(3) TP 8 over PCIe (no NVLink): all-reduces slower than over NVLink
(4) partly supported: FA2 does not support fp8 KV, so vLLM selects another attention backend (FLASHINFER for most
    models, with Q in bf16). Workaround: --attention-backend TRITON_ATTN (source:
    https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/60262)
(5) 8 GPUs in one group, but NVLink connects only 2 of these cards: the group talks over PCIe (slower)
(6) partly supported: No FP8 tensor cores below CC 8.9: per-tensor FP8 (e.g. ModelOpt FP8, min capability 8.0) runs
    through the weight-only MarlinFP8ScaledMMLinearKernel (W8A16, BF16 compute). Triton FP8 MoE requires supports_fp8
    (CC 8.9) and FlashInfer/DeepGEMM/CUTLASS need SM90+, so the FP8 MoE oracle lands on MarlinExperts (weight-only FP8,
    BF16 compute). Workaround: --attention-backend TRITON_ATTN (source:
    https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.31.0/vllm/platforms/cuda.py#L605-L606)
(7) 4 GPUs in one group, but NVLink connects only 2 of these cards: the group talks over PCIe (slower)

Memory per GPU with one request of 32k tokens (≈ 25,000 words ≈ 49 pages): the model's weights and its conversation
memory (KV cache) are computed exactly, the engine's fixed costs are estimated. Margin = room left for more
conversations after that request. Card memory = what CUDA reports (the engine's usable memory; nvidia-smi shows a little
more).
Users: total = concurrent requests ÷ 10% active at once (--active); each gets ≥20 tok/s and the first token within 10 s
(or, if longer, twice the wait for the first words of one typical request alone: half the context, less what the prefix
cache holds).
Accuracy: exact = computed; measured = this model on this card; calibrated = fitted on measurements of this card or of
the same model type (N points); estimated = calibrated on other cards, not verified, or extrapolated; not precise enough
to decide = ±50% or more, or a case not covered.
